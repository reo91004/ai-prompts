# Manuscript Rules

## Scientific narrative

Make the manuscript read as one paper: a clear problem, contribution, method, and set of results that support its central claim. Introduce the purpose and terminology early enough for a reviewer reading it for the first time. Select material by its role in understanding and judging that claim. Retain the complete internal experimental record, including negative results and exploratory follow-up, outside the manuscript.

Remove revision history, debugging chronology, internal variable names, run identifiers, and operational notes from the narrative unless an identifier is scientifically necessary. A paper should not inherit the structure of an experiment log.

## Direct, accurate presentation

Lead with the supported result and describe the conditions in which it was obtained. For example, prefer "We verified pixel equality on two images" to "The verification is limited to two images." State the actual sampling unit and repeat count directly. Preserve numerical values, units, denominators, citations, attribution, and uncertainty.

Remove routine defensive qualifications, speculative objections, and sentences listing unclaimed conclusions. Include a condition wherever it is needed to interpret a method or result accurately. Keep the general Limitations discussion concise and factual. Editing tone must not broaden a claim, change a denominator, turn repeated measurements into independent samples, or remove a result needed to judge the central claim.

Explain a practical alternative, such as storing a latent representation and seed, in terms of its purpose and measured properties. Its usefulness should not be framed as an apology for the chosen method.

## Author and project conventions

Use the professor-approved manuscript as the reference for structure, logic, terminology, and style when it is actually available. A supplied version label such as v3 is project-specific; do not claim to have followed an unseen manuscript. Preserve the user's final-edit regions without rewriting or repeatedly commenting on them.

Match punctuation, quotation marks, and LaTeX conventions to the reference manuscript or venue template. Remove distracting decorative typography without damaging mathematical notation or quotations. Inspect occurrences of `AI` in manuscript text and captions in context; report or correct inappropriate wording without automatically deleting scientific terminology, citations, or author-owned declarations.

## Final read

Read for the central argument and first-reader comprehension. Check that abstract, contributions, method, figures, tables, and conclusion describe the same result. Verify edited facts against their source evidence. Use the project's existing LaTeX build or document-rendering checks when executable manuscript content or layout changes; a small prose edit does not require a new experiment.
